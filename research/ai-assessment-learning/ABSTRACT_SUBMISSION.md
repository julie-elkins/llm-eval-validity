# Abstract submission draft

## Working title

From Correct Answers to Learning Evidence: Designing AI-Assisted Assessment
Around Independent Transfer

## Abstract

Generative AI makes final-answer correctness an incomplete basis for assessing learning. Learners and AI systems can produce correct or fluent responses without showing that a learner can justify an answer, evaluate evidence, or apply a target skill independently. This paper asks whether an evidence-aware assessment view can distinguish those cases more defensibly than a final-answer-only view.

We present a metadata-only synthetic study that tests a protocol for assessing evidence of independent transfer. Independent transfer is represented by a matched task completed without AI assistance; this phase models that outcome synthetically and does not observe learners. The study contains 30 cases across seven failure classes, including correct answers with wrong justifications, fluent but unsupported claims, stale or contradictory evidence, missed uncertainty boundaries, missing constraints, polished negative controls, and evidence-supported cases. Each case is evaluated under two matched views. The final-answer-only view sees the task and answer. The evidence-aware view also sees evidence fields, verification signals, and workflow metadata.

The final-answer-only view agreed with the synthetic reference on 5 of 30 cases (16.7%; 95% Wilson interval [7.3%, 33.6%]). The evidence-aware view agreed on 30 of 30 cases (100.0%; [88.6%, 100.0%]), an 83.3-percentage-point difference within this fixture. These results are not learner outcomes, population estimates, or evidence that AI assistance improves learning. The contribution is a reproducible feasibility protocol for testing whether assessment evidence supports a learning interpretation before human validation. We identify the ethics, privacy, transfer, and missing-data requirements that must be addressed before such validation can begin.

## Keywords

Generative AI; assessment validity; learning evidence; independent transfer;
learning analytics; evidence-centered assessment; human-AI interaction

## Fit statement

This submission addresses the collection's focus on reconstructing the
evidentiary basis of assessment beyond final products and distinguishing
AI-supported performance from independent competence. It contributes a
controlled measurement protocol and a narrowly bounded synthetic feasibility
result, while making the limits of process evidence and the need for human
validation explicit.
