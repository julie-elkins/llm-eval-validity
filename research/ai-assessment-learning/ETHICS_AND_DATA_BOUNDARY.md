# Ethics and data boundary

## Purpose

This study may involve adult volunteers completing learning tasks. The protocol
must establish whether the work requires institutional review, an exemption, or
another documented ethics determination before recruitment begins.

An ethics determination is a documented decision about whether participant
research may proceed under the applicable review process. It is not something to
infer from the study being small or low risk.

The U.S. Common Rule defines research as a systematic investigation designed to
develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge and defines a human subject
around interaction with or identifiable information about a living individual.
This project therefore requires an appropriate local determination before
recruitment; this document does not decide whether the project is exempt.

## Participant protections

Participants must receive plain-language information about:

- what they will do;
- whether AI assistance is permitted;
- what data is collected;
- how long it is retained;
- who can see it;
- whether participation is voluntary;
- how they can withdraw;
- whether they receive compensation;
- that participation has no employment, certification, academic, or hiring
  consequence.

No participant should be asked to disclose employer-confidential information,
private assessment content, customer data, or credentials.

## Public/private boundary

### Public repository may contain

- synthetic task identifiers;
- task-family descriptions;
- protocol revisions;
- rubric dimensions;
- aggregate counts;
- metadata-only reason codes;
- hashes of released artifacts;
- analysis code;
- limitations and ethics statement.

### Public repository must not contain

- participant names or contact information;
- raw participant answers;
- answer keys;
- private certification questions or options;
- reviewer identities;
- raw model or provider responses;
- private prompts;
- employer or customer data;
- credentials, tokens, or access links;
- small-cell outputs that make a participant identifiable.

## AI-assistance boundary

The assistant may only perform the actions declared in the protocol. It may not:

- submit an answer on the participant's behalf;
- access private data;
- infer identity;
- create a high-stakes educational or employment decision;
- hide uncertainty;
- treat a schema-valid response as evidence of learning.

## Publication boundary

The paper must distinguish:

- participant performance;
- AI-assisted performance;
- evaluator inference;
- independent transfer;
- what the study did not measure.

The paper must not call the pilot a validated educational instrument or claim
durable learning unless the design and evidence support that statement.
