# Paper outline

## Working title

From Correct Answers to Learning Evidence: Evaluating AI-Assisted Assessment for
Independent Transfer

## Abstract

State the problem, the distinction between assisted performance and independent
learning evidence, the matched evaluator views, the transfer criterion, the
seven-case synthetic pilot, and the limits of the result.

## 1. Introduction

- AI assistance makes final-answer correctness an incomplete assessment signal.
- Fluent output can conceal unsupported reasoning or absent independent transfer.
- Assessment needs evidence that matches the interpretation being made.
- State the research question and contribution.

## 2. Background and related work

- assessment validity and evidence-centered design;
- generative AI in assessment;
- metacognition and self-regulated learning;
- grounded feedback;
- human-centered and privacy-aware educational AI.

## 3. Research questions and claim boundary

- primary and secondary questions;
- operational definition of independent transfer;
- claims the study can and cannot support.

## 4. Method

- participant boundary and ethics determination;
- synthetic task families;
- baseline, AI-assisted, evaluator, and transfer stages;
- final-answer-only and evidence-aware views;
- evidence rubric;
- reviewer calibration;
- analysis plan.

## 5. Results

- synthetic case completion;
- evaluator-view agreement with the synthetic reference;
- planted unsupported-learning detections;
- human participant and transfer outcomes only in a future ethics-approved
  phase;
- unsupported and contradictory evidence;
- reviewer agreement;
- missingness and uncertainty.

## 6. Failure cases

- correct answer without valid support;
- fluent explanation without transfer;
- evidence mismatch;
- confidence without verification;
- refusal or uncertainty that is safer than an unsupported answer.

## 7. Discussion

- what evidence improved the assessment inference;
- what answer-only evaluation missed;
- implications for AI-assisted assessment design;
- relationship to the current workflow-reliability paper.

## 8. Limitations and ethics

- exploratory sample;
- task-family and transfer-form limits;
- no claim of durable learning unless delayed transfer is measured;
- participant and privacy boundary;
- no use of private certification content.

## 9. Conclusion

Assessment should distinguish a correct answer from evidence that the learner can
apply the skill independently. AI assistance can be part of a valid assessment
workflow only when the evidence boundary and interpretation are explicit.
