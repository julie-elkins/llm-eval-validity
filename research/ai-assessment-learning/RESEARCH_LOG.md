# Research log

## 2026-11-19 — Initial protocol boundary

**Decided:** Build a separate companion study applying the current workflow-
reliability method to AI-assisted assessment and independent transfer.

**Instead of:** Modifying the current ARR paper or treating a correct AI-assisted
answer as evidence of learning.

**Why:** The ARR paper's question is whether provider-constrained workflow
outputs contain usable evidence. The new study asks whether assessment evidence
supports an inference about independent learning. They are related but require
different outcomes and a different venue.

**Costs if wrong:** The repository will contain two related studies and require
clear navigation. That cost is lower than weakening the submitted paper with a
second population and research question.

**Decided by:** Julie Elkins, 2026-11-19.

## 2026-11-19 — Defer human participants

**Decided:** Make the current paper a non-participant synthetic study and defer
human validation until an institution or qualified review body determines the
appropriate ethics route.

**Instead of:** Recruiting adult volunteers before establishing whether the
activity requires review, exemption, or another approval path.

**Why:** The synthetic phase can test whether the rubric detects planted
evidence failures without making claims about real learner outcomes. The human
phase would collect performance data from living people and therefore needs a
formal boundary determination before recruitment.

**Costs if wrong:** The current paper will not contain empirical learner
outcomes, and its external validity—how well it transfers beyond constructed
cases—will be limited. That is preferable to collecting data whose publication
status is uncertain.

**Decided by:** Julie Elkins, 2026-11-19.

## 2026-11-19 — Use independent transfer as the learning criterion

**Decided:** Use a matched task completed without AI assistance as the primary
observable criterion for independent learning evidence.

**Instead of:** Using answer fluency, confidence, explanation length, or an
AI-assisted score as a proxy for learning.

**Why:** Those signals can be produced by a fluent system without demonstrating
that the learner can apply the target skill independently.

**Costs if wrong:** Transfer tasks are harder to design and may underestimate
learning if the new surface form is poorly matched. The protocol therefore
requires task review and explicit limits.

**Decided by:** Julie Elkins, 2026-11-19.

## 2026-11-19 — Public synthetic tasks and adult pilot

**Decided:** Use public synthetic tasks and, if approved by the applicable ethics
process, a small adult volunteer pilot.

**Instead of:** Using private certification material, production traces, minors,
or protected educational records.

**Why:** The study must be inspectable without exposing confidential content or
making employment, certification, or academic decisions about participants.

**Costs if wrong:** Synthetic tasks may limit external validity, meaning how well
the result transfers to other settings. That limitation is preferable to
publishing private or high-stakes assessment data.

**Decided by:** Julie Elkins, 2026-11-19.

## 2026-11-19 — Synthetic baseline frozen

**Decided:** Freeze the seven-case offline synthetic baseline as the current
executable result: final-answer-only agreement 1/7 and evidence-aware agreement
7/7 with the private synthetic reference disposition.

**Instead of:** Treating the result as evidence about real learners, running a
live provider arm, or recruiting participants before an ethics determination.

**Why:** The fixture tests whether the proposed evidence boundary catches
constructed cases where correctness or fluency is not enough to support an
independent-learning inference. Keeping the result synthetic preserves the
distinction between measurement feasibility and learner-outcome research.

**Costs if wrong:** The result has limited external validity and cannot answer
whether people actually learn. That limitation is preferable to making a
human-learning claim from constructed cases.

**Decided by:** Julie Elkins, with Codex implementation support.

## 2026-11-19 — Expand the synthetic pilot before abstract submission

**Decided:** Expand the executable synthetic phase from seven examples to a
frozen 30-case metadata-only matrix before preparing the Springer abstract.

**Instead of:** Submitting the seven-case demonstration as if it were a
standalone learner study or recruiting participants before an ethics
determination.

**Why:** The collection requests empirical or conceptually grounded work on
assessment evidence, durable learning, and transfer. Repeating the seven
failure classes makes the measurement behavior easier to inspect while
preserving the distinction between a controlled feasibility result and a
learner outcome.

**Costs if wrong:** The repeated cases can make descriptive intervals look more
precise than the constructed design warrants. The paper therefore labels the
repetitions as controlled robustness checks and does not treat them as
independent learners.

**Decided by:** Julie Elkins, with Codex implementation support.
