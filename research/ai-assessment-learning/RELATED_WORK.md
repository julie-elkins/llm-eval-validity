# Related work and research gap

## Assessment validity

Assessment validity concerns whether the evidence supports the interpretation
being made from a score. A correct answer can support one interpretation while
failing to support a stronger interpretation, such as durable understanding or
independent competence.

This study carries that distinction into AI-assisted assessment: the response
may be correct while the evidence is insufficient to infer learning.

## Generative AI and learning assessment

The literature increasingly treats generative AI as changing what assessment
can observe. In particular, final-answer correctness may be less informative
when learners can use AI during the task. The study therefore emphasizes
verification, explanation, and independent transfer rather than answer accuracy
alone.

## Grounded feedback and metacognition

Recent work on AI-supported practice exams has studied feedback grounded in
course material alongside confidence and explanation prompts. Those designs are
relevant because they expose evidence and self-regulation signals, but they do
not automatically establish that a learner can transfer the skill without AI.

## Human-centered governance

UNESCO's guidance on generative AI in education emphasizes human-centered
design, privacy, age-appropriate use, ethical validation, and pedagogical
purpose. Those principles support this study's adult-only pilot, public synthetic
tasks, and explicit human-review boundary.

## Executable review and agent-generated research

Recent work on societies of AI agents reinforces the distinction between
publication volume, reproducibility, and scientific validity. *A Society of AI
Scientists* describes an exploratory, observational deployment in which
human-operated laboratories provisioned compute and broad themes while agents
produced studies, code, data, reviews, and discussion artifacts. Its executable
review path checks reported values against cited data fields, but the authors
explicitly distinguish those checks from proving that the code matches the
method, the design isolates the intended cause, or the interpretation is
justified. The work therefore supports this study's emphasis on claim-to-
evidence traceability, negative controls, and separate human judgment; it does
not validate the results of this study or establish a causal benefit of
multi-agent research.

## Research gap

The proposed contribution is narrower than “AI improves learning”:

> When an AI-assisted assessment produces a correct or fluent result, which
> evidence is sufficient to support an inference about independent transfer?

The study connects:

1. assessment validity and evidence-centered design;
2. AI workflow evidence contracts;
3. human reviewer calibration; and
4. independent transfer as a bounded learning criterion.
